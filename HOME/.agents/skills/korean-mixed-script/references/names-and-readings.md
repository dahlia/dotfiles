Names and foreign readings
==========================

Evidence and choice of name
---------------------------

Check the person's own public usage or an authoritative institutional source
for the Han spelling and current name. Consult reliable Korean-language usage
to assess recognition. A user-supplied verified spelling can supply the needed
context; do not repeat questions already answered.

For Chinese, Japanese, and Vietnamese loans, identify the original language
and actual orthography. Do not back-form characters from a romanization or
Hangul pronunciation. For Vietnamese names, Han-Nom sources may be needed.
If competing chữ Nôm spellings exist, prefer an overwhelmingly established
Korean-language form. Otherwise summarize candidates, sources, and uncertainty
and ask the user to choose.

For difficult proper names, use independent subagent research when available
and permitted. Give researchers distinct source scopes and require direct
evidence. Repeated copies of the same unsourced spelling are not independent
confirmation. Stop short of a final choice when the evidence remains unclear;
report the research and ask. If delegation is unavailable, disclose that limit
and investigate with the tools actually available.

An actual Han-character personal name can take precedence over a better-known
Western name, with the familiar name in parentheses. Hong Minhee's specified
case is 黃仁勳(Jensen Huang). Annotate the Chinese-name pronunciation as needed
by the output system; Jensen Huang is a familiar-name gloss, not its reading.

A Western scholar's adopted East Asian name does not automatically displace
the name used among Korean readers. Conversely, 朴露子 is the user's specified
name for 박노자 regardless of whether citizenship is the reason for its use.
Origin, nationality, self-identification, and sustained usage matter together;
neither Korean popularity nor ancestry alone is an automatic selection rule.

For Korean names, do not guess characters from sound or infer a surname from
its frequency. Chinese Wikipedia may supply a lead only when its cited source
actually substantiates the spelling. Read that source. An unknown name stays
in Hangul pending evidence. Do not treat a verified native Korean given name
as an invitation to translate it semantically into Hanja.

Use current names. 唐鳳 is the user's specified Chinese name for Audrey Tang;
do not append a former name as background or add deadnames to a glossary.
Institutional renamings are a separate historical-reference question, not an
extension of a person's deadname rule. Retain source identifiers when needed
to make a reference traceable without introducing a deadname.


Regional form rules
-------------------

Normalize Chinese names under the same Kangxi baseline and explicit personal
preferences as Korean lexical text. Routine use of simplified characters in
a Chinese source does not by itself override this rule. Resolve one-to-many
simplified characters by the actual name, not by a single-character converter.

For modern Japanese people born before Japan's defeat in World War II, use
old forms unless the person explicitly established a particular spelling.
For people born after the defeat, use the person's approved spelling, including
new forms, old forms, and name-specific variants. A modern person's explicit
statement about their own character form takes precedence over the birth-period
rule. Preserve distinctions such as 高 U+9AD8 and 髙 U+9AD9.

The exact date dividing the birth periods has not been chosen. Ask when a
borderline birth date makes a difference. Do not invent a legal registration
spelling, infer a variant from a font, or apply a person's birth-period rule
to a company's founding date.

Apply the relevant principles to countries, places, companies, products, and
organizations, checking their attested names. Use 越南 for Vietnam, with
베트남 supplied as its intended reading where required. This rule does not
authorize inventing Chinese translations for every Western brand or translating
an ASCII product identifier.


Readings
--------

Keep the Han spelling, source language, intended Hangul spelling, and familiar
alternative name distinct. Use Korean loanword spelling rules for local-language
readings. Check established Korean forms and primary pronunciation evidence
rather than mechanically pronouncing each character with Korean Hanja readings.

This does not require replacing established Korean names such as 中國 and
日本 with Mandarin or Japanese transcriptions. Distinguish an established
Korean lexical name from a local-language loan; check the applicable convention
or ask when the choice is unclear.

In HTML-capable Markdown or HTML, use language/script tags on the base and
reading, with fallback parentheses:

~~~~ html
<ruby lang="vi-Hani">胡志明<rp>(</rp><rt lang="vi-Hang">호찌민</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>
~~~~

Use BCP 47 to distinguish language from script. Hanja in Korean prose is not
automatically Chinese. The example deliberately represents Vietnamese using
Han characters and a Hangul transcription. Do not replace its language tags
with Hangulize's three-letter model codes. Choose appropriate language/script
tags for other entries; do not invent unregistered subtags.

When HTML/ruby is unavailable, use 胡志明(호찌민). With an actual configured
converter, its verified reading mapping can provide the annotation. A general
claim that the converter exists is insufficient evidence that a particular
entry, occurrence, or output channel has the right reading.
